What size line array does your event need?

Guest count is the first number we ask for, and the room is the second. A ballroom for 600 and an open field for 600 are two different rigs. These are the shapes most Dallas-Fort Worth events land in.

Under about 250 guestsPoint source PA, no array needed

Rig 2 powered tops · 2 subs · small console

Private dining rooms, boardrooms, ceremonies and small receptions. In a room this size a good point-source PA covers every seat and takes up less of the room doing it. We will tell you when this is the right call rather than sell you an array you do not need, and the gear comes off our sound equipment rental inventory.

250 to 800 guestsGround stacked, four per side

Ballrooms, banquet halls, worship spaces and hotel general sessions. Four Aero 12 cabinets a side sitting on the sub array covers this comfortably, and it goes in without touching the venue rigging. This is the line array rental we do most often in the Metroplex.

Corporate general sessions, university events and large outdoor weddings. Getting the array in the air is what changes the result here. Flown boxes clear the standing crowd, and we can aim the bottom cabinets at the back of the room and the top ones short of the ceiling.

1,500 guests and upFull hang, six per side

Rig 6 Aero 12 per side flown · full sub array · delays as needed

Festivals, concerts and main-stage productions. Six cabinets a side is the top of our hang, and past a certain depth of field we add delay positions rather than push the main array harder. For anything at this scale we want to walk the site or review a venue plan before we quote it.

An outdoor show gives you no reflections to fill in the gaps, so everything the audience hears comes straight out of the boxes. Outdoor events get a bigger system than the same headcount indoors, plus a power plan and ballast or towers if there is no roof to fly from.

Gymnasiums, atriums, hard-surfaced ballrooms and any venue with a balcony. These are the rooms where a pair of speakers on sticks turns into an echo, and where being able to angle each cabinet independently is the whole point of bringing an array.

The short version

What is a line array PA system?

A line array is a column of matched cabinets hung in a vertical curve so their outputs couple into one source instead of fighting each other. A traditional point-source system loses about 6 dB every time you double the distance from the speaker, which is why the front rows get blasted and the back of the room gets mush. A line array cuts that loss across its usable range, so the first row and the last row hear something much closer to the same thing.

Each cabinet in the hang can be angled on its own. That lets us point the array at the audience and away from the ceiling, the back wall and the glass. In a reflective venue such as a gymnasium, an atrium or a hard-surfaced ballroom, that directivity is what keeps a presenter intelligible instead of washing the room in reflections.

The working rule we use: below about 250 guests in a normal room, a point-source PA is the right tool. Above that, or outdoors, or in a long room, an array is what keeps every seat sounding the same.

PA system rental in DFW when two speakers will not cover the room

Most PA system rental searches in Dallas-Fort Worth end at a pair of powered tops on sticks, and for a lot of events that is the correct answer. The calls that come to this page are the other ones: a general session for 900 where the CEO has to be understood in the back row, a festival stage where the sound has to carry past the barricade, a gymnasium where the last graduation turned into an echo.

The difference is not just volume. A bigger point-source system played louder makes the front rows uncomfortable before it makes the back rows clear. An array changes the shape of the coverage rather than the amount of it, which is why we reach for one as soon as the room gets long, tall or full.

Rigging

Flown or ground stacked: your venue decides

Rig 6 per side flown · 4 per side ground stacked · rigging plan supplied

We do both, and we would rather fly it. An array in the air gets above head height, which means the first row is not absorbing the whole system and the back of the room is being aimed at deliberately. Where a venue has rigging points and the load ratings to use them, that is what we do.

Plenty of DFW rooms have no points, or points a rigger will not sign off on. Then we ground stack four Aero 12 per side on the sub array or on lift towers, which gets the boxes high enough to clear a standing crowd. It is a real system either way, tuned the same way.

Your production manager gets a rigging plan and a certificate of insurance naming the venue, sent at intake rather than chased for on the Friday. We advance load in, power and curfew with the venue directly, so nobody discovers a freight elevator problem on the day.

Concerts and festivals

Concert sound system rental and festival sound in Dallas-Fort Worth

Concert and festival work is what a line array was built for. These are the four situations we get called for most, and what changes about the rig in each one.

Outdoor stages with no walls

Outdoors there is nothing to help you. A room gives you reflections that carry sound to the back for free, and a field gives you none of that, so everything the audience hears has to be aimed there on purpose. That is what a flown array does, and it is why an outdoor event of 800 guests can need more system than an indoor event of 1,500.

Festival sound and lighting on one call

Most festival organizers we work with would rather book audio and lighting together than coordinate two crews across one load in window. We run both, so the rig, the stage lighting and the power plan get advanced as one job with one schedule.

Delay towers and front fill

Past a certain depth, a single hang cannot serve the front and the back honestly. Delay towers put a second source further into the crowd, time aligned so it reinforces the main array instead of smearing it, and front fill covers the guests standing closer than the array is aimed. Both are how a big field stays even.

Multiple acts sharing one rig

Support acts, changeovers and a headliner all on the same system means the console has to hold several shows at once and the changeover has to fit in fifteen minutes. We build the input list around every act on the bill and keep an engineer on it all night, so the last band gets the same system the first one had.
