What the cloud looks like on a dance floor

Every clip below is our own footage, shot on the night at a booking we ran in Dallas-Fort Worth. Six rooms, each a different kind of venue: a daylight greenhouse, a grand ballroom, a stone hall, an uplit chapel, a live band on a stage and a rustic barn. Watch what the fog does at floor level, how flat it stays while people move through it, and how the color in the room reads back out of the cloud.

The effect, by the name most people use

Dancing on the clouds

Dancing on the clouds is a knee-deep layer of white fog laid flat across the dance floor. It stays down at floor level, leaves the air above it clear, and holds its edge at the tables. The couple stands above it rather than inside it, which is the whole point: from the front row and from the photographer’s position, the floor disappears and the two of you appear to be standing on a cloud.

It works because the fog is chilled. Warm fog rises and fills a room; cold fog sinks and stays. All four of our sizes do that same job by a slightly different route, three chill the fog by passing it through a basket of dry ice, and the fourth chills it with an onboard ultrasonic water system and no dry ice at all.

People search for this effect under half a dozen names, dancing on the clouds, a cloud machine, low lying fog, ground fog, dry ice fog, a low fog machine for a first dance. They are all the same look. What differs is which machine gets used, how long the cloud holds, and whether anyone is standing there running it.

A dry ice cloud lasts as long as the dry ice load lasts. The size of the ice basket is what sets that.

The one thing nobody tells you before you book
The part that decides everything

How long the cloud lasts

This is the question that matters and it is the one most rental listings leave out. A dry ice cloud machine works by dropping a basket of dry ice into heated water. Fog is produced for as long as there is ice in the basket. When the ice is gone, the cloud stops, and it stops all at once. So the honest answer to “how long does it last” is a number that belongs to your booking rather than to the machine: however long the ice load in that particular machine lasts, at the output level it is being run at.

Which means the size of the ice basket is the specification that governs your event. A machine with a small basket can carry one song beautifully. A machine with a large basket can carry a grand entrance walk, hold through the introductions, and still have ice left for the first dance. Running a small machine hard to cover a long moment is how people end up with a cloud that dies halfway through the chorus.

So it is easier to think of what we own as four sizes of the same effect rather than four machines, a ladder from one defining moment up to all night long. One of them comes to your event. These are the four rungs, smallest load to largest.

The second half of the answer is timing. Dry ice machines need to come up to temperature before they will produce anything, and the ice has to go in close to the moment or it sublimates away doing nothing. So we agree the running order with your planner and your DJ or band in advance, and our technician loads and fires on the cue. The cloud peaks on the beat the song starts on. That is a coordination job as much as an equipment one, and it is why someone stands with the machine instead of leaving it switched on in a corner.

When a moment is genuinely too long for one load, or the floor is genuinely too wide for one machine, we bring two. Machines can be combined for back-to-back effects, one covering the grand entrance and a second held in reserve for the first dance, or run side by side to cover a larger floor evenly instead of pushing one unit past what it can do.

Wedding fog machine rental

What it looks like at a wedding

This is the reason nearly everyone books it, and there are four moments in a wedding where it usually goes.

The effect photographs better than it describes. A knee-deep white layer under a couple, lit from the sides, gives a photographer a clean horizon where there was previously a scuffed dance floor, chair legs and a cable run. It separates the two of you from everything behind you. That is why the first dance photos from a cloud floor look like they were taken somewhere else entirely. Our write up on fog effects for a wedding entrance and first dance covers how the cue is timed against the music.

Moment 01

Grand entrance

The cloud runs ahead of you down the walk so you arrive out of it rather than into an empty floor. This is the moment most likely to need the large machine, entrance walks are longer than people expect once introductions are added.

Moment 02

First dance

The classic. The cloud is laid down as the song starts and holds through it. Timing is everything here, which is why our technician takes the cue from your DJ or bandleader rather than guessing at the intro.

Moment 03

Parent dances

Back-to-back with the first dance, and a common place for a cloud to die early. Covering both from one load is a capacity question, and it is what the larger basket is there for.

Moment 04

Ceremony exit or last dance

A low layer under a recessional, or under the final song of the night when the floor is full. Quieter uses of the effect, and both of them photograph beautifully.

The cloud also reads color. If we are already providing uplighting or dance floor lighting, the fog picks the color up and carries it across the floor, which is a considerably better look than white fog sitting under colored light that never reaches it. When the whole room is designed together, the effect reads as part of the room rather than as an add-on.

Most couples book this alongside DJ services or as part of a wedding package, which is also the easiest way to get the cue right: when the person calling the fog and the person playing the song work for the same company, the cue lands where it should.

Same logic, higher stakes, because a video crew resets and reshoots. We will usually recommend the continuous machine for the base layer and hold a dry ice unit for the one hero shot that needs a genuinely dense cloud.

Questions we get every week

Dry ice and low lying fog, answered

How long does a dancing on the clouds effect last?
For as long as the dry ice load lasts, and no longer. A dry ice machine makes fog by dropping ice into heated water, so output continues while there is ice in the basket and stops when there is not. That is why basket size is the specification that matters, and why we own the effect in four sizes rather than one machine: the 8 lb size is built for one defining moment, the 12 lb size covers back-to-back dances, and the 30 lb size carries a long entrance and still has ice left. The fourth size uses no dry ice at all and has no stopping point. Rather than quote you a number that depends entirely on how hard the machine is run, we ask what moments you want covered and bring the machine that covers them.
What is the difference between dry ice fog and a regular fog machine?
Temperature, and therefore where the fog goes. A standard fog or haze machine produces warm fog, which rises and fills the room, useful for making light beams visible, useless for a cloud effect. Dry ice fog is chilled, so it sinks and stays flat at ankle-to-knee height, holds a defined edge, and dissipates without hazing the room or drifting over the tables. If you want the floor to disappear, you need the cold kind. If you want the air to glow around a lighting rig, you want the warm kind, and those are two different machines.
Do you deliver, set up and run the machine, or is this a pickup rental?
We deliver, set up and run it. A technician brings the machine, positions it for the room, sources and loads the dry ice, takes the cue from your DJ or bandleader, operates the effect and packs it down afterwards. Everything from the ice to the strike is on us. Most low lying fog listings in Dallas-Fort Worth are twenty-four-hour box rentals with the ice and the setup left to you; that is a genuinely different product, and it is worth checking which one you are comparing us against.
Do I need to buy the dry ice?
We handle it. We source it, transport it and load it, and we bring the right quantity for the machine and for the number of moments you want covered. Dry ice needs the right supplier, the right quantity, safe handling and correct timing, and it sublimates away whether you are using it or not, so ordering it the morning of your wedding is a job worth keeping off your own list. Handling all of that is part of the booking.
Is low lying fog safe for guests, the dance floor and the photographer?
It is a widely used effect and we run it with a technician present for that reason. The fog is water vapor chilled by carbon dioxide, it stays low, and it clears on its own. Our technician places the machine so the output lays flat across the floor rather than pooling at one edge, keeps the floor in view for the whole effect, and manages output if the room is behaving differently than expected. Floors can become slightly damp with heavy use, so we place and run it with that in mind, and we will always talk through anything specific to your venue or your guests beforehand.
Will the fog set off the venue's smoke detectors?
We will not tell you it cannot happen, because any airborne effect can interact with a detector. What we do is clear the effect with the venue in advance and, where the venue uses one, with their alarm monitoring company, and then place the machines away from detectors and return air vents. Many venues have a defined process for fog effects and need advance notice; some restrict dry ice entirely, which is one of the reasons we own a machine that does not use it. Tell us the venue early and we will handle that conversation before the date rather than on it.
Can we use the cloud effect for the grand entrance and the first dance?
Yes, and this is the situation that decides which machine we bring. Two separated moments need either one large load or two machines. We can combine machines for back-to-back effects, with one covering the grand entrance and a second held in reserve for the first dance, or run two side by side to cover a larger floor evenly rather than pushing a single unit past what it can do. Tell us every moment you want the cloud on and we will build the plan around all of them instead of hoping one load stretches.
Do you rent low lying fog machines outside of weddings?
Regularly. Quinceanera waltzes, milestone birthdays, corporate grand entrances and product reveals, photo shoots and video shoots. The corporate reveals usually run on our DMX machine so the effect fires inside a programmed lighting cue, and shoots usually run on the continuous machine because a camera crew resets more often than a dry ice load allows. Same fleet, same technician, same approach. We ask what the moment is and bring the machine that fits it.
