Special Effects Rental

Which special effect suits the moment you are planning?

Most people arrive here with a picture in their head and no name for it. The white blast over a dance floor, the silver fountains behind a couple, the beams cutting across a ballroom and the cloud that sits on the ground are four separate machines with four separate rigs. Here is what each of these extras does, so you can point at the one you meant. Each creates a different kind of visual moment, and most rooms want two of them rather than four.

CO2

CO2 cannons and guns: a white plume

Rig 2 handheld CO2 guns · 2 CO2 cannons with LEDs on DMX · CO2 supplied

A burst of liquid CO2 leaves the nozzle and flashes into a column of white vapor that shoots overhead and vanishes in a few seconds. It is cold rather than hot, it leaves the floor dry, and it lands on a beat. This is the effect on the page with no separate page of its own, so it gets the long version further down.

Best for Club nights, DJ drops, raves, birthday entrances and hype moments in a corporate general session.

Cold sparks

Cold sparks: silver fountains

Rig Cold spark machines · licensed pyrotechnician · permit, book 4 to 6 weeks out

Cold spark machines throw a fountain of silver sparks several feet into the air from a granule that burns far cooler than traditional pyrotechnics. Guests read it as fireworks indoors. It is the effect people mean when they say sparklers for the first dance. It is also the one effect here that has to be permitted and run by a licensed pyrotechnician, so it needs the longest lead time of anything on this page.

Best for First dances, grand entrances, cake cuttings, quinceañera waltzes and stage reveals.

Lasers

Lasers: beams across the room

Rig Laser projectors · run with haze · aimed above head height

Lasers draw sharp beams and shapes through the air and can sweep an entire ballroom from one position. They want something in the air to be visible at all, which is why lasers and haze get quoted together. Aiming and safety zones are set during load in and held all night.

Best for Nightclubs, raves, concerts, dance floors and large corporate reveals.

Fog and haze

Fog and haze: air you can see light in

Rig Hazers for beams · high density foggers for a burst

Haze is a thin, even suspension that makes every beam in the room read as a solid shaft of light. High density fog is the opposite job: a heavy burst behind a doorway for a silhouette entrance, gone within a minute. Both machines are covered in full on their own page.

Best for Ballrooms with a lighting package, grand entrances, club nights and camera work.

Clouds

Dancing on the clouds: a cloud on the floor

Rig Low-lying fog · dry ice or water based · held for the song

Low-lying fog stays at ankle height instead of rising, so a couple appears to be standing on a cloud while their faces stay clear for the camera. Dry ice gives the denser, whiter version of it. This one has a dedicated page as well, because the coverage and the timing matter more than the machine does.

Best for First dances, quinceañera waltzes, sweet sixteens and theatrical reveals.

CO2 Cannon And CO2 Gun Rental

CO2 cannons and CO2 guns: a white plume on the drop.

Rig 2 handheld CO2 guns for your hands · 2 CO2 cannons with LEDs on DMX, technician run · CO2 supplied

Our CO2 guns and cannons create plumes of cold white smoke that shoot overhead and clear in seconds. Liquid CO2 travels from a tank through a hose to the nozzle, and the pressure drop at the nozzle flashes it into vapor and ice crystals. That is what you see: a bright white column that reads as an explosion on camera and disappears before the next bar of the song.

We carry two of each shape, and they are used in two different ways. The two handheld CO2 guns go in your hands. We charge them, show you the trigger and stand close by, then the couple, the guest of honor or whoever you want holding them fires the plume where they want it, straight up over the dance floor or out across the crowd. Most of the best photographs we get all year come from that thirty seconds. The two mounted cannons are ours to run. They have LED lights in the head and sit on DMX with the rest of the lighting rig, so the plume comes out lit in a color, and a technician fires them from the board on cues the DJ calls and the timeline sets. That is what puts the blast on the drop, on the entrance or on the reveal instead of a second behind it.

The reason CO2 gets used indoors so freely is that the plume is cold and it leaves the room clean. The plume is cold, the floor stays dry and the room is clear afterwards, so it suits venues that hold a firm line on flame and residue. What it does need is height above the nozzle and a clear path for the plume, which we check when we walk the room.

We bring the CO2, set the tanks, run the hose so it stays out of the walkway, and stay with the rig for the length of the event. The cannons fire from our board on DMX, cued off the DJ and the run of show. The handhelds are yours for the moment you want them, with our technician on them either side of the hit, loading, checking pressure and taking them back afterwards. When we are running your audio and lighting as well, the cannon cue is shared across the room, so the blast, the light and the drop land as one thing.

Cold Spark Machine Rental

Cold sparks: indoor sparkle fountains for the moment everyone films.

Cold spark machines create cascades of silver sparks that rise several feet and fall away, and they do it at a temperature low enough that many venues allow them where real pyrotechnics are refused. Guests read the effect as fireworks indoors. Photographers love it because the sparks light faces from below while the couple stays in focus.

These are the machines people are describing when they ask about sparklers for a first dance, cold sparklers for a wedding, or spark fountains for a stage entrance. Placement, the safe distance to the nearest guest and clearance overhead all get agreed with your venue before the date instead of worked out on the night.

Lead time Permit approval takes 4 to 6 weeks · book cold sparks first

Cold sparks are handled as a permitted effect. A licensed pyrotechnician operates the machines at your event, and the permit has to be approved by the authority having jurisdiction over your venue before we can fire them. That approval runs four to six weeks, so cold sparks are the first thing to lock in on a date, ahead of every other extra on this page. Tell us the venue and the date early and we start the paperwork the same week. If your date is inside that window, say so anyway and we will tell you honestly whether it can still be permitted in time, and what the alternatives are if it cannot.

Laser Light Show Rental

Lasers: beams and shapes across the whole room.

A laser rig draws hard edged beams, fans and shapes through the air, and one position can cover an entire ballroom. Because a laser beam is only visible where it hits something, we run lasers through haze so the beams read across the room instead of only at the wall. Aiming, safe zones above head height and the operating position are set during load in and held for the night.

Lasers have their own page.

Programming, the effects a laser can draw, and how a laser show is timed to music are covered on our laser light show page. Lasers and haze are quoted together often enough that we treat them as one line on the plan, and both come from the same crew on the same load in.

Fog And Haze Machine Rental

Fog and haze: the air that makes every other effect visible.

Haze is a thin suspension you barely notice until a beam passes through it, and then every moving head, pin spot and laser in the room reads as a shaft of light. High density fog does the opposite job. It is a heavy white burst you use as one moment, usually behind a doorway for a grand entrance, and it clears within a minute.

Because haze is what lets lasers and beam fixtures exist in the air, it gets added to most effects packages by default. Machine choice, venue rules and the difference between fog, haze and smoke are all set out on our fog machine and haze rental page.

Dancing On The Clouds

Dancing on the clouds: low-lying fog for a first dance.

Low-lying fog is chilled so it hangs at ankle height across the dance floor instead of rising, which gives you the dancing on the clouds look: a couple standing on a white cloud with faces clear above it. Dry ice produces the denser, whiter version and has been the theatrical standard for decades; a water based machine gives you a continuous cloud with no dry ice to source on the day.

How much floor a cloud covers, how long it holds and how it is timed against the song matter more here than the machine does, which is why the clouds effect keeps a page to itself.

The full clouds service

Coverage, dry ice handling and timing are covered on our dancing on the clouds page. Weddings book this alongside cold sparks often: a cloud under the first dance, then sparks as the song lands.

What We Clear Before The Date

Every venue has rules about effects, and we settle them in advance.

Special effects are the part of an event most likely to run into a house rule, and finding that out on the day is expensive. So before your date we take the venue name, contact the room, and agree what is allowed, where the rig sits and what paperwork they want. Most rooms in the Metroplex we have worked before, so the answer usually comes back the same week.

Detectors and alarm monitoring

Fog and haze can trigger smoke detectors, so we clear the effect with the venue and with the alarm monitoring company where there is one, and place machines away from detectors and return vents.

Ceiling height and clearance

CO2 plumes and spark fountains both need room above the nozzle. We measure the height over the exact spot the effect fires from, because that is the number that governs the plume.

Floor plan and safe distance

Where guests stand, where the cake table sits and how far the nearest chair is all shape the placement. That plan goes to the venue before load in.

Overhead rigging and drapery

Drape, greenery, chandeliers and low truss all change what an effect can do underneath them. We look up during the walk through, not during the reception.

Permits, licensing and insurance

Cold sparks are permitted and fired by a licensed pyrotechnician, and permit approval runs four to six weeks. Every rental carries full insurance, with a certificate for your venue whenever they ask for one, supplied ahead of load in.

Power and cable paths

Effects share circuits with audio and lighting, so we plan the power draw and run hose and cable where guests will walk without stepping over anything.

Questions

Special effects rental questions.

What special effects do you rent in Dallas-Fort Worth?
CO2 cannons and handheld CO2 guns, cold spark machines, laser light shows, fog and haze machines, and low-lying fog for the dancing on the clouds effect. Every one of these extras is delivered, rigged, cleared with your venue and staffed by one of our technicians for the length of your event, then loaded out afterwards. The handheld CO2 guns are the exception on operation: we hand those to you for the moment you want them.
How do CO2 cannons work at an event?
Liquid CO2 runs from a tank through a hose to the nozzle. The pressure drop at the nozzle flashes the liquid into vapor and ice crystals, which is the white plume you see shooting overhead. It rises, spreads and disappears within a few seconds, so it works as a hit on a beat instead of continuous atmosphere. Our two mounted cannons carry LED lights in the head and run on DMX, so the plume can come out lit in a color that matches the lighting cue.
Are CO2 cannons safe to use indoors?
CO2 is a cold effect that leaves the floor dry and the carpet clean, and venues that refuse open flame will frequently still allow it. What it needs is height above the nozzle and a clear path for the plume, plus a ventilated room. We check both when we walk your venue and we agree the placement with the room before your date.
What is the difference between cold sparks and CO2?
Cold sparks throw a silver fountain upward from a granule and read as fireworks indoors, which suits a first dance or a cake cutting. CO2 creates a white column of cold vapor that vanishes in seconds, which suits a drop, a reveal or a club room. Sparks are the romantic effect and CO2 is the hype effect, and plenty of events run both at different points in the night.
Will fog or haze set off the smoke detectors at my venue?
It can, which is why we settle it before your date. Manufacturers state plainly that atmospheric effects can trigger detectors, so we clear the effect with your venue and with the alarm monitoring company where there is one, then place machines so the output stays away from detectors and return vents. Some rooms allow haze but restrict heavy fog, and we will tell you which yours is.
What is included in an effects rental?
Delivery, placement, testing and collection come with every effects booking. It arrives with our crew, gets placed where the venue has agreed, is tested before doors, and a technician stays with it through your event. The one thing you do get your hands on is the handheld CO2 guns. We load them, show you the trigger and stand with you, and you fire them yourself, which is how most people want that moment to go. The cannons, the lasers, the sparks and the fog stay on our board, cued off the DJ and the timeline.
Can I book more than one effect for the same event?
Yes, and most events do. A wedding might take a low-lying cloud for the first dance and cold sparks as the song lands, while a club night takes haze all evening with CO2 on the drops. Booking them together means one crew, one load in and one contract, and the cues get programmed against each other rather than fired by different people guessing.
How far in advance should I book special effects?
For CO2, lasers, fog, haze and clouds, two to four weeks is comfortable, mostly so there is time to clear the effect with your venue before the date, and shorter notice is often workable in a room we already know. Cold sparks are the exception and need longer: the permit takes four to six weeks to approve, so book those as soon as you have a date. Send us the date and the venue name and you will get a straight answer back.
Do you cover Fort Worth and the rest of DFW?
Yes. We work roughly a forty mile radius from the mid-cities between Dallas and Fort Worth, which covers Arlington, Plano, Frisco, Irving, Southlake, McKinney, Grapevine, Richardson, Denton and everything folded in among them. If your venue sits further out, ask anyway and we will tell you honestly whether it works.
Do cold spark machines need a permit in Dallas-Fort Worth?
Yes. We run cold sparks as a permitted effect with a licensed pyrotechnician operating the machines, and the permit has to be approved by the authority having jurisdiction over your venue before the date. Approval runs four to six weeks, which makes cold sparks the longest lead time of anything on this page, so book them first. CO2, lasers, fog, haze and low-lying fog do not carry that permit requirement, though every venue still has its own house rules that we clear in advance.
