AV Production, In Plain English

What “AV production” covers.

It is a phrase that gets used as though everyone agrees on it, and they do not. When we quote AV production for a corporate event, this is what is inside it: four jobs, all of which happen whether or not anyone names them.

01. Planning the room before anyone is in it

Reading the floor plan and the run of show, working out where the system has to go so that sightlines, coverage and cable runs all survive contact with the actual venue, and agreeing a load-in window with whoever owns the loading dock. This is the part that decides whether event day is calm.

02. Building and testing the system

Rigging and cabling the audio, video and lighting, powering it in a way the venue’s circuits can support, then line-checking every input (every microphone, every laptop, every playback source) long before the first person walks in.

03. Operating it live

A technician on the console for the whole event, riding levels as speakers change, cutting between sources, catching the lapel that was clipped to the wrong lapel, and handling the five minutes of the schedule that never survive first contact.

04. Getting out cleanly

Striking the system at the end without turning the room into a construction site while guests are still finishing drinks, and leaving nothing behind for your team to return, ship or explain to the venue the following morning.

Event Technology Services

Event technology, run by the people who installed it that morning.

Event technology services is the broader version of the same job: everything in the room that has to be powered, connected and working at a specific minute. Presentation switching and slide advance, confidence monitors, hybrid connections for remote presenters, wired and wireless audio distribution, video record feeds, and the network-dependent pieces that quietly decide whether a remote panelist joins on time.

The difference between event technology that works and event technology that becomes the story of the day is almost never the equipment. It is whether the person who built the system is still in the room when something changes, and at a real corporate event, something always changes. A speaker arrives with a different deck. A panel moves from four chairs to five. The keynote runs eleven minutes long and the breakouts have to absorb it.

We staff for that. The technician who loads in is the technician at the console, so there is no explaining the setup to a second crew halfway through the day.

Conference & Meeting AV

Every room type in a conference has a different problem.

A conference is rarely one AV job. It is a general session, several breakouts, a boardroom, a lobby and an evening reception, all running on overlapping schedules with the same crew moving between them. We run conference AV in Dallas end to end, from the first load-in to the last case going back on the truck, and we quote it as one production rather than five rentals.

General session and keynotekeynote AV Dallas

Rig line array or point source · projection · stage lighting · FOH tech

The main room, and the one everything else is judged by. Line array or point-source sound depending on the depth of the room, large-format projection or LED-lit screens, stage and podium lighting, presentation switching, and a technician at front of house for the whole session. Keynote AV is where we build in extra headroom, so the opening remarks land the way the speaker rehearsed them.

Breakout roomsmeeting AV services

Rig self-contained sound · display · 1 handheld and 1 lavalier per room

Smaller, faster and easier to get wrong, because breakouts usually turn over on a fifteen-minute gap. Self-contained sound, a display or projector, a wireless handheld and a lavalier per room, all pre-set and labeled so a session change does not require a technician sprinting between three doors.

Boardrooms and executive meetingsboardroom AV

Rig tabletop mics · display · clean feed for remote

Tabletop microphones with even pickup around the table, a display everyone can read, and a clean, quiet feed for whoever is dialing in. Discreet is the specification here: the AV should be invisible and the meeting should not open with ten minutes of connection troubleshooting.

Ballrooms, galas and award nightscorporate event production

Rig podium mic · band or DJ system · follow spot · lighting

Speech, music and lighting have to coexist, usually with a podium, a band or DJ and an awards run. How Event Day Works

Five steps, and you are only in two of them.

This is the sequence for essentially every corporate event we run, from a forty-person quarterly meeting to a full conference general session. The scale changes; the shape does not.

Step 01

Advance

We review the run of show, the venue and the floor plan, confirm what the house can and cannot supply, and coordinate the load-in window with the venue directly.

Step 02

Load-in and setup

We arrive early, build the system, dress the cable so nothing crosses a walkway, and run full line checks on every microphone, laptop and playback source.

Step 03

Rehearsal

Speakers test their microphones and their slides before doors open. Presenters who have done this before ask for it; presenters who have not are grateful for it.

Step 04

Showtime

A technician operates sound, video and lighting throughout, adjusting as speakers, panels and the schedule change. Nobody from your team is at the console.

Step 05

Teardown

We strike quietly and completely once the room clears. Nothing is left for your team to return, ship or explain to the venue in the morning.

Corporate AV FAQ

The questions planners ask.

If yours is not here, send it with your inquiry. We would rather answer it before the quote than after.

Are you an audio visual company or an equipment rental company?
Both, but in that order. Dallas Event Audio is a full-service audio visual company serving Dallas-Fort Worth: we specify, deliver, install and operate complete AV systems for corporate events. Equipment rental is part of that, but it is never sold on its own: every rental includes setup and an on-site technician, because that is what makes the room work.
What corporate AV services do you offer?
Sound systems and microphones, projection, screens, displays and confidence monitors, presentation switching, stage and room lighting, follow spot and logo projection, live streaming and hybrid connections, recording, DJ services, and the technicians to run all of it. For most corporate events these are quoted together as one production rather than as separate rentals.
Do you provide on-site technicians?
Always. There is no version of our service where equipment arrives without a technician. Our crew loads in, builds and line-checks the system, runs rehearsal with your speakers, operates sound, video and lighting for the whole event, and strikes afterwards. The technician who built the system is the one at the console, so nothing has to be explained to a second crew mid-event.
What does AV production mean for a corporate event?
In practice it means four things: planning the room before anyone is in it, building and testing the system, operating it live as the schedule changes, and striking it cleanly at the end. Equipment is only one input to that. When we quote AV production we are quoting all four, which is why the number covers a working room rather than a list of gear.
Can you handle a large conference end to end, general session and breakouts?
Yes, end to end. General sessions are a regular part of what we do: line array or point-source sound scaled to the depth of the room, large-format projection, stage and podium lighting, presentation switching, and front-of-house operation for the duration. Multi-room conferences with breakouts running alongside the main room are quoted as a single production with one crew and one schedule, from the advance call through load-out.
Do you cover keynote AV and breakout room AV with the same crew?
Yes. We treat the keynote room and the breakout rooms as a single production. The same crew builds the general session and every breakout in one load-in, labels each room to its own schedule, and covers them through the day, which is what keeps a session change on time when a breakout runs long.
Do you offer live streaming and hybrid support?
Yes, and the audio is the part that makes it worth doing properly. We patch the stream feed directly from our mixer rather than relying on a camera microphone, so remote attendees hear the same processed, De-Feedback’d speech as the room. Multi-camera setups, remote presenter connections and recording are all available. There is more detail on the live streaming page.
Do we have to use the venue’s in-house AV?
Usually not. Most hotels and conference centers in Dallas-Fort Worth allow outside AV vendors, and many that restrict certain elements still allow the rest. Send us the venue name with your inquiry and we will confirm their policy before you commit to anything, then deal with their operations team on load-in, dock access and paperwork directly.
Are you insured? Can you provide a COI for our venue?
Yes. We are fully insured and routinely provide certificates of insurance naming the venue as required. Send us the venue’s requirements with your booking and we will have the certificate issued and sent to them directly, well ahead of your load-in date.
Can you support a recurring meeting series?
Yes, and it is some of our favorite work. Quarterly all-hands, monthly trainings, annual kickoffs and recurring board meetings all get easier once the room is documented: power, acoustics, load-in timing, what the house system can be trusted with. You can book event by event or set us up as a standing AV partner for the series; either way the same crew comes back.
What areas do you serve?
Dallas, Fort Worth and the surrounding Metroplex, roughly a forty-mile radius from our base in Hurst, which covers Dallas, Fort Worth, Irving, Plano, Frisco, Arlington, Grapevine, Southlake, Las Colinas, Addison, Richardson and the rest of the mid-cities. Events further out are possible; ask us when you send your date.
How far in advance should we book?
Earlier is better, particularly for conference season and December, but we would rather hear from you late than not at all. Short-notice bookings are often workable. What genuinely helps is sending the run of show and the venue as early as you have them, because the advance work is where most event-day problems get solved.

Send the date, the venue, the room or rooms, roughly how many people and what has to happen: presentations, panels, awards, a stream, a party afterwards.
