Fog And Haze Machine Rental

Which fog machine do you need: haze, high density fog or low-lying fog?

Fog covers three separate machines. A hazer makes your lighting beams visible, a high output fogger fills a room on a drop, and a low-lying machine lays a cloud across the dance floor. Getting that choice right is most of what this page is for, so here is the difference in plain terms.

Haze

Haze: beams you can see

Machine Chauvet Hurricane Haze 2D or 4D · water based · DMX

Haze is a thin, even suspension that makes light visible. On its own you barely notice it. The moment a moving head, a gobo wash, a pin spot on the cake table or a laser fires through it, every beam reads as a solid shaft of light. Haze is what makes a lighting package photograph the way it looked in the proposal, and guests read it as atmosphere rather than smoke.

Best for Ballrooms, chapels, corporate general sessions, and any room where lasers or moving heads are in the rig.

High density fog

High density fog: a moment on a cue

Machine Froggy’s Fog Titan 1200 · 1,300 W · variable output

High density fog works the other way around. It is a heavy, opaque burst you use as a single moment: a grand entrance, a DJ drop, a first look, a Halloween walk through, or a photo and video setup that needs the background gone. It rolls in thick, does its job and clears.

Best for Grand entrances, club nights, Halloween events, concert sets and camera work.

Low-lying fog

Low-lying fog: a cloud on the floor

Machine Chauvet DJ Cumulus or ADJ Entour Ice · dry ice or water based

Low-lying fog stays on the floor. It is the dancing on the clouds look, a knee deep cloud that hangs across the dance floor for a first dance or a quinceañera waltz, with the couple standing above it. That machine has a page of its own, and this page carries the rest of the fog and haze inventory.

Best for First dances, quinceañera waltzes, theatrical reveals and anything photographed close to the ground.

Fog Machine For A Wedding Entrance

Fog machine for a wedding entrance in Dallas-Fort Worth.

A grand entrance is what most people are picturing when they search for a fog machine rental for a wedding, and it is worth being clear about which machine gives you the shot you have in your head, because two very different looks get called the same thing. There is a longer write up in fog effects for a wedding entrance and first dance.

A high density fog burst behind a doorway gives you the silhouette. The wedding party walks out of a wall of white, the room cannot see past it until they arrive, and the fog clears within a minute. A low-lying cloud on the floor gives you the walking on air version instead, where the fog holds at ankle height and every face stays clear for the camera.

Which one your venue allows and which one your photographer wants are the two questions worth settling early. Send us the room, the doorway and whether a photographer or a videographer is shooting it, and we will spec the machine, the placement and the cue so it fires on the beat rather than near it.

Haze Machine Rental

Haze machine rental: Chauvet Hurricane Haze 2D and 4D.

Hazers Hurricane Haze 2D, 1,200 cfm · Hurricane Haze 4D, 4,300 cfm

Both of our hazers are water based and run Chauvet High Performance Haze Fluid (HFG), which Chauvet describes as non toxic, non flammable and unscented, and which will not stain fabric or leave residue on a dance floor.

The Hurricane Haze 2D puts out 1,200 cfm from a 0.7 gallon tank, heats up in about five minutes, draws 533 W and gives you two channels of DMX over 3 pin XLR. It ships with a digital display and a 15 foot wired timer remote. At 18.7 lb it is the right hazer for a ballroom, a chapel or a corporate general session, where you want the beams visible while the room still reads clear to the eye.

The Hurricane Haze 4D is the larger machine: 4,300 cfm, a 0.9 gallon tank, a two minute heat up, 1,120 W and the same two DMX channels. Use it when the room is big, the ceiling is high, or the HVAC is strong enough to eat the output of a smaller hazer.

Because both are DMX addressable, we run them from the same console as your lighting and hold the haze at one set density all night instead of chasing it with bursts. That is also why a haze machine rental and a lighting package quote well together.

High Density Fog Machine Rental

High density fog: the Froggy’s Fog Titan 1200.

Machine Titan 1200 · 1,300 W · five-liter reservoir · variable 0 to 100 percent

The Titan 1200 is our burst machine: 1,300 W, a five-liter drop in reservoir, roughly a four minute heat up, and a reheat time Froggy’s rates at under ten seconds after a full cycle, which is what lets it hit repeatedly through a set instead of dying after the first blast. Output is variable from 0 to 100 percent and it runs from a wired remote, an optional wireless remote or a built in timer.

We pair it with Froggy’s Fog Bog Fog extreme high density fluid. It is water based, made from deionized water and pharmaceutical grade, FDA approved ingredients with no glycerol or glycerin, and it produces a very white, very dense fog with a high index of refraction and a slow evaporation rate. That is the fluid you want for a white out effect, for hiding scenic elements, for strobe work and for camera work.

Bog Fog is dense, and that is the point of it. It suits a concert, a club night, a Halloween event or a shoot, and it is usually too much for a plated dinner in a hotel ballroom. If you are unsure which side of that line your event falls on, tell us the room and the moment and we will spec it honestly.

Low-Lying Fog And Dry Ice

Low-lying fog and dry ice: dancing on the clouds.

Low-lying fog is a cloud that stays on the floor instead of rising, and two machines produce it. The Chauvet DJ Cumulus makes the effect with no dry ice at all, using an onboard ultrasonic agitator to turn distilled water into a fine mist that chills the fog so it hugs the floor. The ADJ Entour Ice is the theatrical version, dropping dry ice into heated water the way stage productions have done it for decades, built as a wheeled, insulated, DMX controllable machine.

Dry ice fog reads denser, whiter and colder than the water based version, and it hugs the floor harder, which is why it is still the standard for a first dance, a quinceañera waltz or a theatrical reveal photographed close to the ground. Dry ice has to be sourced fresh on the day and handled safely, and that part is ours.

Because low-lying fog is a whole package rather than a single machine, the full detail has its own page: coverage, how long a cloud holds, what the dry ice service includes, and how it is timed against the song.

Matching The Machine To The Room

Which machine suits your room and your event.

The room decides this more than the effect does. Ceiling height, HVAC, whether there is a plated dinner in progress and what the venue allows all narrow it down before anyone talks about output. Here is how it usually lands.

Hotel ballroom, plated dinnerLight haze only

Machine Hurricane Haze 2D · low set level on DMX

A plated dinner needs the lighting to read without the room looking smoky. One 2D hazer, held at a low DMX level and started before doors so it has time to even out, gives you visible beams over the tables and across the dance floor while the room stays clear to the eye. High density fog in this room is the wrong call, and we will say so before you book it.

Church or chapel ceremonyCleared with the venue first

Machine Hurricane Haze 2D · venue and alarm approval

Chapels are where clearing the effect with the venue matters most, because many of them have their own detectors and their own house rules. Where haze is allowed, a low level is plenty to give a beam of light down the aisle some shape. Where the venue says otherwise, we tell you early instead of on the day.

Grand entrance or revealFog machine for a wedding entrance

Machine Titan 1200 · burst on a cue

This is a single moment, so it wants the burst machine rather than continuous output. We place the Titan behind the doorway or the reveal, fire it on the cue and let it clear. When we are also running your sound and lighting, that cue is shared across the whole rig, so the fog, the music and the light hit as one thing.

Nightclub, rave or concertHaze plus bursts

Machine Hurricane Haze 4D · plus Titan 1200 for hits

Club rooms want both. The 4D holds a constant haze so every beam, laser and strobe reads all night, and the Titan 1200 adds density on the drops. Both run on DMX from the lighting console, so the operator has the fog on faders alongside everything else.

Outdoor tent or patioWind dependent

Machine Hurricane Haze 4D · sided tent recommended

Open air pulls haze apart quickly, so the honest answer outdoors depends on the wind and on how enclosed the space is. A sided tent holds an effect well. A fully open patio usually will not, and we would rather tell you that than take the booking and hope the evening is still.

Photo or video shootCamera work

Machine Titan 1200 with Bog Fog · or 2D for beam work

Shoots split into two jobs. Bog Fog through the Titan 1200 gives you a white out background or hides what should be out of frame, and a light haze from the 2D gives depth and visible light shafts without fogging the subject. A technician holds the density where the camera wants it between takes.

Halloween walk throughHeld for hours

A haunt needs density held for a whole evening rather than one burst. The Titan runs on its built in timer with a technician on site refilling fluid, so hour four looks like hour one. Low-lying fog underneath adds the floor layer if the walk through has a graveyard or a lake scene.

Fog Machines And Smoke Detectors

Fog machines and smoke detectors: what we clear with your venue.

Every manufacturer of every fog and haze machine, Chauvet included, states in the manual that atmospheric effects can set off smoke detectors, and every machine needs clearance around it for ventilation. That conversation is a routine part of the booking, and it is one of the reasons a technician comes with the machine. It is also why we ask for the venue name early: we have worked most rooms in the Metroplex before, and we usually know their rules on haze already.

What is the difference between fog and haze?
Haze is a thin, even suspension that makes light visible, so beams from moving heads, lasers and follow spots read as solid shafts while the room still looks clear. Fog is denser and more visible in itself, either as a heavy burst for a moment like a grand entrance or as a low-lying cloud that sits on the dance floor. Haze is atmosphere for the whole evening; fog is usually a moment.
Do you rent haze machines?
Yes. We run the Chauvet Hurricane Haze 2D at 1,200 cfm for ballrooms, chapels and corporate general sessions, and the Hurricane Haze 4D at 4,300 cfm for larger rooms, high ceilings and aggressive HVAC. Both are water based, both run Chauvet High Performance Haze Fluid, and both are DMX addressable so the haze is held at a set density from the lighting console.
Will fog or haze set off the smoke detectors at my venue?
It can, which is why we handle it before your date rather than on the night. Manufacturers state plainly that atmospheric effects can trigger detectors, so we clear the effect with your venue and with the alarm monitoring company where there is one, and we place the machine so the output stays away from detectors and return vents.
