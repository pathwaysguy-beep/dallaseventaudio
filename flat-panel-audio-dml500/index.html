The Technology

What a flat panel speaker is, and what makes the DML500 different

Panel Bending wave DML · carbon fiber and Nomex honeycomb · four neodymium motors · 165 degree coverage

A conventional loudspeaker moves air with a cone. A piston pushes forward and back, and the sound leaves the front of the box in a beam that gets narrower as the frequency rises. That is why a room covered by cone speakers has a loud spot in front of each one and a dull spot between them, and why the person sitting off to the side hears less of the top end than the person in the middle.

A flat panel speaker works on a different principle. Instead of a cone, it uses a stiff, light panel that is excited into bending waves across its whole surface. The panel does not move as one piece. Thousands of small vibrational modes are spread across it at once, and the sound leaves as a diffuse wavefront rather than as a beam. In the Flat Panel Audio DML500 that panel is a carbon fiber and Nomex honeycomb composite, driven by four neodymium motor structures, inside an enclosure slim enough to hang like a picture frame.

The practical result is 165 degrees of horizontal coverage that stays intelligible right out to the edges. Speech holds together in a room where a cone box would be beaming past half the audience, and the level falls off far more gently as you walk away from it, so the front row is not being punished to reach the back row. It is a PA speaker, so it carries a program at real event levels rather than being a decorative background music panel.

Panel Versus Cone

Low-profile panel speakers compared with the visual footprint of a speaker on a stick

Four differences that decide whether a flat panel loudspeaker is the right call for your room. Each one points to a specific set of jobs a panel does well, and we will tell you honestly whether yours is one of them.

01

Coverage across the room

Panel 165 degrees horizontal · diffuse wavefront

A cone speaker narrows as frequency rises, so the seats off axis lose consonants first. The panel spreads a diffuse field across 165 degrees, which keeps speech readable at the sides of a room without a second speaker aimed there. In a wide, shallow room that is often the difference between one flat panel and a pair of boxes on stands.

02

How the level drops with distance

Panel Gentler falloff · smaller front to back spread

A point source loses roughly six decibels every time the distance doubles, which is why the first two rows get hammered so the last two rows can hear. The panel's falloff is gentler, so the range between the nearest listener and the furthest one is narrower. A gallery talk stays comfortable at the front and audible at the back off one system.

03

A low-profile panel with no visual clutter

Panel Low-profile enclosure · wall mount, stand or ground stack

This is the reason most people call about it. The DML500 is a thin panel that can sit flat against a wall or on a low-profile base, instead of a black trapezoid on a tripod at head height. In a room that has been styled by a designer, or photographed for a brand, a speaker with almost no visual footprint is worth a lot.

04

Where a cone speaker still wins

Panel Add subwoofers for music and dancing

A flat panel is a mid and high device. It will not produce the low end a dance floor or a live band needs on its own, and it is not what we would send to a rave. Pair it with subwoofers and it covers music well. For a DJ set, a band or a party where the bass is the point, we will say so and quote you a conventional rig instead.

Where It Fits

Panel speakers for events in rooms that will not take a speaker on a stick

The call almost always starts the same way: the venue, the designer or the client has said no visible speakers, and somebody still has to be heard. These are the rooms where that comes up in Dallas-Fort Worth, and what we do in each one.

Museums and galleriesOpenings, donor events, curator talks

The art is the reason people are in the room, and a speaker on a tripod in front of a hung work is a problem before anybody turns it on. Flat panels go against a wall or on low-profile bases below sightline, cover the floor evenly for a talk that moves around the space, and come down without touching a wall surface. Conservation staff care about what gets rigged and where, and we advance that with them rather than arriving with a plan.

Historic venues and houses of worshipStone, glass, long reverb

Older rooms are difficult speech environments and they are rarely willing to be rigged. Hard stone and glass keep energy in the room long after the word has finished, so a beamy speaker pointed at the ceiling turns a sermon into mush. A diffuse panel field puts less energy into the reflective surfaces overhead and more into the seated area, which is why intelligibility comes up even though the room did not change. Where a wireless microphone is in play, our AI-powered De-Feedback system runs on every channel, which is what buys usable gain in a room that wants to ring.

Corporate rooms with sightline rulesProduct launches, showrooms, press

A launch stage gets designed months before anyone asks where the audio goes, and the answer is usually that there is nowhere for it. Flat panels sit inside a set, against a back wall or flanking a screen at a height that does not cross a camera. For anything being filmed or streamed, that matters twice, because a speaker in shot is a speaker in the recording that goes on the company's channel afterward.

Upscale retail and hospitalityTrunk shows, activations, hotel salons

Retail activations and hotel function rooms are styled to within an inch of their lives and are usually small enough that a full PA is overkill. A panel or a pair covers the space for a host, a short program and background music, tucks against a wall out of the traffic pattern, and reads as part of the room rather than as gear that showed up.

Weddings at venues that ban visible speakersCeremonies, chapels, garden rooms

A growing number of DFW wedding venues have a written rule about visible black boxes in the ceremony space, and the aisle photograph is the reason. Flat panels solve the picture and the coverage at once: even level across seating that is wider than it is deep, no tripod in the frame, and the officiant's wireless microphone running through the same system. Reception audio is a separate conversation, because the dance floor needs low end that a panel does not make.

Conference breakouts and panel sessionsMeetings, seminars, training rooms

Breakout rooms are wide, low ceilinged and full of people sitting well off center. That geometry suits a flat panel and gives a pair of tops on sticks trouble. Panels along the front wall give the whole room the same intelligibility, and the presenter is not competing with the sound of their own voice arriving late from a speaker behind them.

Questions

Flat panel speaker questions

What is a flat panel speaker?
A flat panel speaker replaces the cone of a conventional loudspeaker with a stiff, light panel that is excited into bending waves across its whole surface. Thousands of small vibrational modes spread across the panel at once, so the sound leaves as a diffuse wavefront rather than as a beam. That gives wide, even coverage from a thin enclosure that can sit flat against a wall. The Flat Panel Audio DML500 uses a carbon fiber and Nomex honeycomb panel driven by four neodymium motor structures, and covers 165 degrees horizontally.
How is the DML500 different from a regular PA speaker?
Three ways that matter on an event. Coverage is much wider and stays intelligible off to the sides, where a cone speaker narrows as frequency rises. Level drops off more gently with distance, so the front row is not overwhelmed to reach the back row. And the enclosure is a thin panel rather than a box on a tripod at head height, which is why it gets specified in rooms that have been designed or photographed. The trade is low end: a panel is a mid and high device and wants a subwoofer under it for music.
Can you use flat panel speakers for a live event, or are they only for background music?
They run live events. The DML500 is a PA speaker, so it carries speech and program material at real event levels rather than sitting at background volume. We use panels for gallery openings, donor events, curator talks, wedding ceremonies, product launches, worship services, breakout sessions and press conferences. For a dance floor or a live band we add subwoofers, or we quote a conventional system instead and tell you why.
Why do flat panel speakers work better in reverberant rooms?
Hard stone, glass and plaster hold sound energy in the room long after the word is finished, and a beamy speaker aimed into those surfaces feeds the problem. A diffuse panel field puts a larger share of its energy into the seated area and less into the reflective surfaces overhead, so more of what a listener hears is direct sound and less is reverberant wash. Intelligibility comes up without the room being changed, which is the only option in a building nobody is allowed to alter.
Our venue does not allow visible speakers. Does this solve that?
Usually, yes, and it is the usual reason people call us about panels. The DML500 is thin enough to sit flat against a wall or on a low-profile base below sightline, so there is no tripod at head height in the aisle photograph or in a camera shot, and the visual clutter in the room is close to nothing. Send us the venue's rule in writing along with a photo of the room, and we will confirm before you book whether panels satisfy it or whether the venue is asking for something else entirely.
How many flat panels does a room need?
It comes from the shape of the room and where people are sitting, not from the guest count on its own. A wide, shallow room often needs fewer panels than you would expect, because 165 degrees of coverage from one position reaches seats that would need a second cone speaker aimed at them. A long, narrow room or a space with an obstruction in the middle needs more. Send the room and the seating plan and we will tell you what covers it.
Do you deliver and set up, or do we pick the equipment up?
We deliver, place, tune, run and collect. Delivery, setup, an on site technician and breakdown are included in every booking, and there is no customer pickup and no drop off. Panels are placed for coverage, cable is run and dressed clear of walkways, microphones are rung out before doors and a technician stays with the system through the event. Every rental is fully insured and venues that need a certificate of insurance get one before load in.
