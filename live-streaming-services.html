Live Streaming Services Dallas

The sound is where most live streams fall apart.

Sit through a badly streamed conference and the cameras are almost never the problem. The picture is fine. What drives the remote audience away is a presenter who sounds like they are speaking from the back of a parking garage, a panel where only the person nearest the camera is intelligible, and a room whose air handling is louder than the microphone. Every one of those is an audio problem being solved with a camera.

We come at this from the other direction. Dallas Event Audio has been running live sound in this metroplex since 2001, so when we stream an event, the microphones are already ours, already mixed, and already processed. The stream gets a direct feed from the mixing console, the same signal we send to the speakers in the room, patched into the video switcher on a dedicated audio input. The cameras supply the picture and the console supplies the sound.

The video side is genuinely good: up to four Sony 4K cameras cut live by an operator, so the stream follows the speaker, the slides and the room instead of staring at one wide shot for two hours. But the reason people stay watching is that they can understand what is being said. If you want the wider picture of what we run on the sound side, start at our audio services.

Signal Path

Same room, same microphone, two ways to reach your remote audience.

One route runs across the room into a camera, which hears the presenter along with the air conditioning and the row behind you. The other runs into our console, gets processed channel by channel, and reaches the switcher as a board feed.

The Difference

Your microphones reach the stream as a board feed.

This is the whole argument for hiring an audio company to run your stream, so it is worth being specific about it. A camera has a microphone on the front. Point it at a stage forty feet away and it records the stage, the HVAC, the ice machine, the coffee service at the back and the two people whispering in row four, all at roughly the same volume, because a microphone does not know what you care about.

We take the stream audio from our own microphones instead. The presenter’s lapel, the handheld going around the Q&A, the podium microphone and the panel table are all on our console. Each one is treated individually, then summed into a stereo feed and patched into the video switcher on a dedicated audio input. What the stream carries is the mix, the same one the room hears, minus the room.

On top of that, every microphone runs through our AI-powered De-Feedback processing. It watches for the frequencies that are about to ring and pulls them down before they do, which means we can run gain high enough for a soft-spoken presenter to be genuinely clear without the system howling. Remote viewers get the benefit twice over: the speech is louder relative to the room, and it is not sitting on top of the low-level ring that makes long streams exhausting to listen to.

Why the remote audience drops off

Attention on a stream is far more fragile than attention in a room. Someone sitting in your ballroom has social pressure, a table, a coffee and nowhere else to be. Someone watching from a desk has a browser tab and no obligation whatsoever. The moment they have to work to understand a sentence, they leave. Clean, processed, feedback-free speech is what keeps a remote audience with you through a long session.

Wedding Live Streaming

Wedding streaming is a smaller part of what we do, and it carries a lot of weight. There is usually a specific reason it is being booked, a grandparent who cannot travel, family overseas, someone who is not well enough to be in the room, and there is one take.

We keep the camera positions unobtrusive and work to whatever your officiant, venue and photographer have already agreed. Venue Requirements

What we need from the room

Streaming has three genuine dependencies. We check all of them before the day rather than discovering them at eight in the morning.

An internet connection we have testedWired if at all possible

This is the one that decides whether a stream succeeds, and it is almost always the venue’s house Wi-Fi that fails it, not because it is slow at nine in the morning, but because it is being shared with four hundred phones by ten. We ask for a wired connection to the streaming position wherever the building can provide one, and we test the actual upload speed from that actual jack in advance rather than trusting the number on the venue’s information sheet.

A camera position with a real sightlineAnd an agreement that it stays clear

Cameras need somewhere to be that is not behind a pillar, a floral installation or a service door that is about to have twelve plated dinners carried through it. We walk this with you or the venue beforehand and mark the positions on the floor plan, because a tripod that has to move during a keynote is a shot that is gone.

Power at the streaming positionOn a circuit nobody else is using

The switcher, the encoding laptop, the console and the cameras all need clean, uninterrupted power. The failure mode is never dramatic: somebody plugs a warming tray into the same circuit and the breaker takes the stream down mid-sentence. We identify a dedicated circuit as part of the advance and run our own distribution from it.

A little time before doorsEnough to build and test

We need the load-in window to include a real test, the stream going live to your platform, checked from a second device outside the building, with a microphone open and somebody listening. Building the rig is the quick part. Confirming that what leaves the room arrives at the other end takes the rest of the window, and we plan the schedule around it.

FAQ

Live streaming questions

Do you offer live streaming services in Dallas?
Yes. We stream corporate conferences, general sessions, town halls, panels, awards nights, hybrid meetings, webinars and weddings across Dallas, Fort Worth, Plano, Irving, Frisco, Southlake, Arlington, McKinney, Richardson and the surrounding metroplex. Every booking is delivered, built and operated by our technician. Delivery, setup and an operator are included in every stream.
What platforms can you stream to?
Whichever one you already use. Zoom and Microsoft Teams for internal and hybrid meetings, YouTube Live and Facebook Live for public events, Vimeo for gated or branded streams, Twitch for entertainment audiences, and any corporate platform that gives us an RTMP destination and key. The stream goes out on your account, so the registration list, chat, analytics and recording all stay in systems your organization already controls.
How many cameras do you use?
Up to four Sony 4K cameras, and we pick the number based on the event rather than a package. One is right for a single presenter in a fixed position. Two is what most corporate events book: a wide and a tight, or a stage camera and the slides. Three or four is what a panel, an awards night or a multi-speaker general session needs so the cut can follow whoever is speaking.
Why does your stream audio sound different from other live streams?
Because it does not come from a camera. Your microphone receivers run into our AI-powered De-Feedback system before the console, so background noise, room reverb and any ring are stripped out first. The console then gates, compresses and EQs each channel starting from a signal that is already clean, and that finished mix is patched directly into the video switcher as a board feed. Remote viewers hear processed speech rather than a microphone on the front of a camera picking up the stage, the air conditioning and the back of the room all at once.
What is De-Feedback and why does it matter on a stream?
It is AI-powered processing that watches for the frequencies about to ring and pulls them down before feedback happens. In the room it means we can run microphone gain high enough for a soft-spoken presenter to be clearly heard. On a stream it matters twice as much, because a remote listener has only the audio to work from, and a room full of people to keep them there, so speech that is loud, clear and free of the low-level ring is what keeps them watching.
Can you run a hybrid event where half the audience is remote?
Yes, and that is the format this setup suits best. A hybrid event is one event with two audiences, which means microphone coverage planned for both, a room mix and a stream mix that are related but not identical, and somebody whose job during the session is watching what the remote audience is receiving. Because we are running the room audio as well, both feeds come off the same console.
Do we get a recording afterwards?
Yes. The session is archived on the platform you streamed to, in your own account, ready to download, trim or repost. If you want a proper multi-camera video record of the event as well as a stream, that is a different service, see our corporate photo and video page, where we deliver raw footage with a 24-hour turnaround.
Do you also shoot corporate photography and video?
Yes. We cover corporate events with photography and videography and deliver the raw image files and raw video footage directly to you, typically within 24 hours. There is no editing pass. You receive the original files, so your own team or editor can do whatever they need with them without waiting on a post-production queue. It is a separate booking from streaming but it works on the same event and the same load-in.

Three things, all of which we confirm in advance: an internet connection we have tested from the actual streaming position, preferably wired rather than house Wi-Fi; a camera position with a sightline that will still be clear once the room is dressed and staffed; and power on a circuit that is not shared with catering equipment. We also need the load-in window to include enough time to take the stream live and check it from outside the building before doors.
