Sound Quality

Four things that decide how a private room sounds

Sound quality at a luxury event comes down to four things: how evenly the room is covered, what the low end does, whether speech stays clear, and how much headroom the system has left when the night gets loud. Here is how we handle each one.

Even coverage The same sound wherever a guest stands

How 165° horizontal and vertical per panel · line array column

A common complaint about sound quality at a private event is that the room has a loud half and a quiet half. It happens because a conventional cabinet throws a narrow vertical pattern, so a guest standing near the speaker gets more level and more top end than a guest at the far wall, and turning the system up for the far wall punishes everyone near it.

A DML500 panel covers 165 degrees horizontally and 165 degrees vertically, and several panels hung as a line array behave as one tall source, so level falls off gently down the length of the room. A guest at the bar and a guest by the window hear close to the same thing, and people can talk at a normal volume anywhere in the space.

Low end you can hear as pitch A bass note, played at the right level

How BassBoss ZV28 · flat to 23 Hz ±3 dB · tuned to the room

Plenty of systems are loud underneath and still sound wrong, because the bass arrives as one wide thump rather than as the note that was played. A subwoofer that rolls off early ends up working above its range, and it starts adding its own character to everything below the vocal.

The ZV28 runs flat down to 23 Hz ±3 dB, which is below the lowest note on a five string bass, and it holds that at a level where a typical cabinet has already turned into a rumble. On a listening party or a producer playback session you hear it on a single sustained sub line: the pitch moves, and you can follow it.

Speech that stays clear Every word from the podium to the back table

How Diffuse wavefront · AI-powered De-Feedback · levels set at the mic position

A luxury venue is usually built out of stone, glass and polished plaster, and those surfaces send sound straight back into the room. Speech is where that shows up first, because the reflection arrives a moment behind the direct sound and smears the consonants that carry the meaning.

Flat panels put less energy into those surfaces to begin with, which is where intelligibility in a hard room starts. The De-Feedback system holds down the frequencies that want to ring, and our engineer sets the level for every microphone with somebody standing at the speaking position before doors. A toast reaches the back table while the system still sounds quiet in the middle of the room.

Headroom and room tuning Loud with the system working comfortably

How Rig sized above what the room needs · measured and tuned before doors

A system running near its limit sounds tense long before it sounds distorted. The top end hardens, the low end compresses, and a room that felt good at dinner gets tiring two hours later. We size the rig above what the room needs so it spends the night with headroom in hand.

Then it gets tuned to the space it is standing in. Our engineer measures the room, corrects for what the surfaces are doing rather than for how the speaker measures in a warehouse, and sets the system up so it moves from conversation level to full playback in one move with the character staying the same.

The System

A DML500 line array over BassBoss ZV28 subwoofers, tuned for a private room

One sound system, built for luxury events where the audio has to be full range and the hardware has to stay out of the sightline. Here is what goes in the room and why each piece is in there.

01

Flat Panel Audio DML500 panels in a line array

Tops Bending wave DML · carbon fiber and Nomex honeycomb · 165 degrees horizontal and vertical

The DML500 is a bending wave panel rather than a cone speaker. A stiff carbon fiber and Nomex honeycomb panel is driven by four neodymium motors, and thousands of small vibrational modes spread across it at once, so the sound leaves as a diffuse wavefront rather than as a beam. Coverage is 165 degrees horizontally and 165 degrees vertically, out of an enclosure thin enough to sit against a wall. A conventional cabinet throws a narrow vertical pattern, so people standing get a different sound from people seated. The panel covers both at once, which is why a room where guests are moving around does not have hot spots and dead spots.

Hung in a line array configuration, several panels work as one tall source. Level drops off more gently with distance than it does from a single point source, so the guests nearest the system are not being punished to reach the guests at the far wall. In a long room that is the difference between a conversation people can have and a party they have to shout across.

02

BassBoss ZV28 subwoofers underneath

Subs Dual 18 inch drivers per cabinet · flat to 23 Hz ±3 dB · ground stacked out of sightline

Panels are a mid and high device, so the low end comes from the subwoofers. The BassBoss ZV28 carries two 18 inch drivers in one cabinet and runs flat down to 23 Hz ±3 dB. That is below the lowest note on a five string bass, so a synth sub or a kick drum arrives as a pitch you can hear rather than as a thump in your chest, and the cabinet holds that at a level where a typical sub has already turned into a rumble. On a listening party or a producer playback session that control is the whole point, because a bass note has to read as a note rather than as pressure.

The cabinets go on the floor, usually tucked against a wall or behind something structural, so the part of the rig that cannot be hidden is also the part nobody looks at.

03

Shure wireless microphones with De-Feedback

Mics Shure wireless handhelds and lapels · AI-powered De-Feedback

Every private event has a moment where somebody talks: a host, a producer introducing a record, an artist thanking a room. We bring Shure wireless handhelds and lapel microphones, and they run through our AI-powered De-Feedback system, which finds and holds down the frequency that is about to ring before it becomes a squeal. That matters more in a luxury venue than in a ballroom, because the marble and glass that make the room look expensive are the same surfaces that make it ring.

The panels help here before the De-Feedback system does anything. A flat panel radiates diffusely instead of aiming a lobe of energy back down the room at an open microphone, so the rig starts with a lot of gain before feedback, and the De-Feedback system takes care of what is left. Between the two the system is feedback resistant enough that ringing the room out is not part of our setup.

We put fresh batteries in on our own schedule, and we set the level for every microphone with a person standing where the speaker will stand.

Private Event Spaces

What we tune the system for

The same panels and subwoofers get set up differently depending on what the room is for. These are the private events we get called about most, and what changes in each one.

Listening parties Album and single playback

Rig DML500 line array · 2 ZV28 subs · engineer at reference level

A listening party lives or dies on whether the record sounds the way the artist heard it in the studio. We tune flat rather than for a party curve, get the level right for the size of the room, and hold it there, so the low end that was mixed in is the low end people hear. Between plays the system drops to conversation level in one move and comes back up when the next track starts.

Panels are placed so the room reads evenly from wherever a guest is standing, which is the awkward part of a listening event: nobody sits down, and the crowd moves between tracks.

Private DJ sessions Small floor, real system

Rig DML500 line array · 2 to 4 ZV28 subs · DJ booth feed

A private DJ set in a home, a penthouse or a members club needs a system that feels like a club at a volume that will not get the building called. The subwoofers do the work here, and the DML500 panels keep the top end even across a floor that is usually wider than it is deep. We can take a feed from your DJ, or supply the booth and the CDJs if that is easier.

If a noise limit or a neighboring tenant is part of the equation, tell us at the quote stage and we will build the rig around it rather than turning you down at eleven.

Music producer events Playback, demos and industry rooms

Rig DML500 line array · 2 ZV28 subs · multiple source inputs

Producer sessions bring a room full of people who listen for a living, which is the toughest audience a rental system gets. We set the system up so the mix translates: even coverage, controlled low end, and enough headroom that a loud passage does not compress into mush. Several sources can be patched at once, so a laptop, an interface and a turntable all go through the same tuned system without anyone crouching behind a rack mid-event.

Intimate live sets Acoustic, duo and small band

Rig DML500 line array · 2 ZV28 subs · input list and monitoring

A stripped back performance in a private room is a real live sound job in a space with none of the room a venue gives you. Send us the input list and we build to it: vocal microphones, instrument inputs, monitoring for the performers, and a front of house mix that works for a crowd standing eight feet from the artist. Our live music and band setup scales down to this without changing the way it is run.

Private galas and receptions Speeches, then a floor

Rig DML500 line array · 2 ZV28 subs · handhelds and a lapel

A private gala runs two shows in one night. During dinner and speeches the priority is that every word carries to the back table, which is where the panel coverage and the De-Feedback system earn their place. After the program the same rig turns into a dance system with the subwoofers brought up, so there is no changeover in front of the guests.

Brand and press events Launches under embargo

Rig DML500 line array · 2 ZV28 subs · podium mic and press feed

An embargoed launch has the same discretion requirements as a private party and a harder audio brief, because what is said at the podium has to be captured cleanly as well as heard in the room. We provide a clean feed for whoever is recording, run the podium and handheld microphones through the same feedback control, and keep the system out of the camera frame your team is working in.

The Room

Private event spaces that are difficult to put a sound system in

Luxury venues in Dallas share a set of problems. Hard surfaces everywhere, because stone and glass photograph well. Ceilings that are either far too high or low enough that a line array has to be flown carefully. Sightlines a designer has already committed to. And frequently a rule that nothing may be drilled, taped or leaned against a wall.

Flat panels answer most of that at once. A DML500 has almost no visual footprint, it can hang, sit on a low-profile base or ground stack, and its diffuse wavefront puts less energy into the hard reflective surfaces that make a marble room ring. We walk the space before the event wherever the venue allows it, and we come back with a plan that names where each panel and each subwoofer goes.

Homes, penthouses, private clubs, galleries, hotel suites, rooftop terraces and event houses across the Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex are all rooms we have worked in. If your venue has a house engineer or a preferred vendor list, we work inside it.

Some rooms are better served by conventional cabinets than by panels, and we carry those as well. If a tented terrace or a warehouse space wants a conventional PA speaker sound system instead, we will say so when we size the rig rather than sell you the panels for their own sake.
